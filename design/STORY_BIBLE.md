# SEVENFALL: THE WAR FOR CREATION
## Narrative bible 1.1 | The Seventh Silence and 75-mission structure
**Status:** Narrative specification, not an implemented campaign. **Continuity:** Expands [GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md) without replacing the original three factions, nine subfactions, C&C harvesting economy or the existing eight-mission titles per campaign.

> The Seventh Seal opened, and Heaven fell silent. The armies kept marching.

## THE PROMISE

This is a three-sided war in which every playable side has a convincing reason to fight, a lie it has told itself, and a price for winning. Humans are not automatically righteous; angels are not uniformly ruthless; demons are not uniformly loyal to Hell. Salvation, sovereignty and freedom are different words. Sevenfall asks which one is worth a world.

**Logline:** At the end of the Seven Seals, seven reality-wounds called Seamfalls fracture Earth. Their crystal deposits, Veilglass, let three invading civilizations rewrite the world. The Celestial Host announces a merciful but coercive quarantine. The Infernal Legions claim an older right to rule. The Last Dominion of Man refuses either verdict. A lost covenant says Earth can only be permanently claimed with the consent of those who live there. A conspiracy has deleted that clause from both armies' orders.

**Central theme:** A command, however ancient or radiant, is not the same as moral authority. A revolt, however justified, is not automatically freedom. Mortal choices matter precisely because they cannot be undone.

**Genre and tone:** Gothic biblical-apocalyptic science fantasy; ruined modern Earth; military RTS tragedy, espionage, logistical warfare and impossible celestial spectacle. Divine silence is a mystery, **not** an assertion that the God of any real religion is evil, absent, dead or secretly a game boss.

## 2. THE SECRET HISTORY

### Before the fall: the First Covenant
Long before written human history, a group of celestial custodians called the **First Watch** crossed the Veil to teach mortals how to read the architecture of reality. Some taught medicine and music, others weapons and conquest. Their intervention was catastrophic. Their descendants and the humans who protected them became known in shattered fragments of history as the **Witness Kin**, later confused with stories of the Nephilim.

The First Watch was recalled, imprisoned or scattered. But before the Veil was repaired, a covenant was inscribed in sevenfold form in a mechanism called the **Witness Ledger**. Its operative law was not a spell granting humans superiority; it was a *limit on outside authority*: neither Heaven nor Hell may permanently remake the living Earth unless its people freely give consent.

The Ledger was never a physical book to be recovered intact. It was divided among seven ancient sites, encoded partly in the fabric of the realm and partly in the memory practices of the Witness Kin. This avoids the narrative becoming a search for a single magical child or weapon. Ordinary communities, not one chosen bloodline, retain the final deciding power.

### The Seventh Silence
The first six seals brought conquest, war, scarcity, death, testimony and cosmic terror to an already collapsing Earth. When the seventh opened, the expected final command did not come. For a moment the entire sky went silent. The Celestial Host's chain of authorization stopped receiving fresh confirmation. Earth cracked at seven major **Seamfalls**; Veilglass grew where creation's underlying laws were exposed.

The Seraphic Synod received an apparent emergency edict: secure the Seamfalls, remove unreliable mortal control and seal the planet beneath a permanent **Edict of Dawn**. An opposing order appeared in Hell, authorizing the infernal crown to claim Earth as settlement for an ancient debt. Neither order was wholly authentic.

### The lie
**Chancellor Vhal**, master of the Court of Whispers, discovered fragments of the Witness Ledger and forged two sets of contradictory divine legal instructions. The Synod believes it is saving humanity by denying humanity command. The Iron Legions believe the world is a lawful spoils-of-war province. By making both forces contest the same mining fields, Vhal gathers enough Veilglass to open the **Ninth Mouth**, a gateway large enough to overthrow Hell's rulers and make himself the arbiter of consent.

His great error: consent obtained by terror, deception or captivity is void. Neither he nor any of the rival powers understands the law until living people refuse them face-to-face.

**No one confirms why the Throne is silent.** The story never equates an angel's order with the Creator's direct will. Many angels hear no such order and continue defending civilians; many demons reject Vhal's version of freedom. The controversy belongs to institutions and characters, not to a judgment on real faith.

## 3. SEVEN SEAMFALLS AND VEILGLASS

Seven primary ruptures form the campaign's geographical spine; lesser map deposits occur wherever fissures spread. The existing **Broken Choir - Proving Ground** remains the first reusable multiplayer/test map and gains lore as the abandoned outskirts of a shattered sanctuary city.

| Location | Story function | Battlefield design |
|---|---|---|
| **Broken Choir** | First major contest, buried records of the First Watch | Symmetric surface arena, exposed crystal and central enriched basin; existing proving-ground identity |
| **The Glass Fields** | Industrial origin of Veilglass harvesting | Wide approaches, rich but vulnerable expansion deposits |
| **The Ashen Causeway** | Mass refugee flight, infernal siege corridor | Long highway, timed civilian routes and chokepoints |
| **The Sable Crown** | Infernal iron foundries and rival houses | Fortified ridges, gateways and dangerous approaches |
| **The Salt Meridian** | Poisoned waterways and disputed water supply | River crossings, contamination warnings and naval potential |
| **The Empty Gate** | Failing celestial threshold, order versus mercy | Vertical fortress, shielded choke points and evacuation lanes |
| **The Harrow of Dawn** | Seventh, deepest scar; location of the reconstructed Ledger's control chamber | Three-way endgame, finite mining fields and timed superweapon preparation |

**Resource law (already in existing game design):** Veilglass is one **physical, finite, harvestable mineral**, with rough and enriched grades. The Angel **Celestial Gatherer** is durable and slow and unloads at a **Sanctification Crucible**. The Human **Industrial Harvester** is balanced and unloads at a **Fracture Refinery**. The Demon **Infernal Devourer** is fast but fragile and unloads at a **Maw of Want**. All three generate ordinary production credits via physical harvesting, not passive points, sacrificial souls or capture circles. Existing prototype resource values are rough Ore 30, enriched Gems 55, subject to play balance.

Each side covets the Harrow's enriched node for a different purpose:
- **Heaven:** fire the **Edict of Dawn**, locking breaches and possibly trapping the whole mortal world under involuntary guardianship.
- **Humanity:** deploy the **Lazarus Protocol**, a precision orbital/ground network that can cut every external conduit but might destroy the last safe refugee corridors.
- **Hell:** open the **Ninth Mouth**, letting infernal armies and rebellious servants cross the boundary in vast numbers, at the risk of making the world uninhabitable.

The three existing superweapon names, their faction identities and their projected RTS counterplay are retained. They are **future systems**, not implemented 0.2 features.

## 4. WHO ACTUALLY FIGHTS?

### Heaven: the Celestial Host
The Host is an institution, not a species hive mind. Its higher orders do not all agree about the silence. The game's **nine angelic choirs** provide visual and command inspiration, not nine extra selectable subfactions.

- **Seraphic Synod:** led by **High Legate Seraviel**. A luminous tribunal of purification. She sincerely believes that locking away mortal weapons and governments will save more lives than negotiation. Her nightmare is discovering that her edict was forged. **Current exclusive unit:** Seraphim. Future doctrine: devastating radiant assaults with limited static defenses.
- **Order of the Gate:** led by **Custodian Orathiel**. Threshold guardians who defend evacuations and prevent passages being seized. They oppose both a demonic conquest and an indiscriminate Synod purge. **Current exclusive unit:** Gatekeeper. Future doctrine: wards, shields and strong fortresses with slow deployment.
- **The Unshackled:** led by **Captain Maeron**, a former archivist cast out for refusing the new orders. His followers have not become demons. They have surrendered their claim to automatic obedience. **Current exclusive unit:** Penitent. Future doctrine: rapid unconventional raiding, weaker wards.

**Other current Angel roster woven into missions:** Watcher scouts and garrison infantry; Judicator anti-armour detachments; Aegis Engines; Radiant Spires; Choir Citadels; Heavenforges; All-Seeing Halos; Restoration Daises; Celestial Archives and Sky Sanctums. Aircraft and additional angel beings are future assets, not available at the 0.2 rules stage.

**Heaven's playable moral choice:** enforce sacred *procedure* even if the person issuing it is wrong, or preserve the people the law was supposed to protect.

### Humanity: the Last Dominion of Man
The Last Dominion is a fragile war coalition, not a single real-world nation or faith. Its founding principle is that no one may surrender another person's life or liberty without their consent. It frequently violates that principle under pressure.

- **Bastion Directorate:** led by **Marshal Mara Voss**. Keeps food routes and cities alive through martial rule, massed Bulwark tanks and Siege Cannons. She can become the leader who saves millions or the one who decides they no longer need freedom.
- **Free Earth Union:** led by **Engineer-General Idris Awan**. Independent shelters, elected councils, Wasteland Rangers and mobile industrial Harvester convoys. Every rescued settlement makes the Union stronger and harder to coordinate.
- **Ashen Covenant:** led by **Doctor-Archivist Elian Vey**. A human consortium of theologians, field surgeons and researchers preserving witness testimonies alongside relic engineering. Its Ash Templars wield anti-entity science. Some demand that experiments on Witness Kin should never be allowed, even to save a city.

**Current Human roster woven into missions:** Rifleman, Rocketeer, Bulwark Tank, Siege Cannon, Wasteland Ranger, Ash Templar; Veil Generators, Fracture Refineries, Survival Barracks, Motor Pools, Radar Domes, Service Depots and Technology Centres.

**Humanity's playable moral choice:** independence cannot be purchased by treating civilians, volunteers or the Witness Kin as expendable components in a machine.

### Hell: the Infernal Legions
Hell is both a realm and a layered imperial war machine. **Devils**, in Sevenfall's original taxonomy, are contract-writing elites with domains, titles and debts; **demons** are a broad array of infernal beings including constructed shock troops, beasts, parasites and primordial entities; **fallen angels** are beings who left the Host and are not automatically members of Hell. The apocalyptic **Dragon** is an unseen claimant invoked by Infernal propaganda. Neither his identity nor the metaphysical position of Satan or Lucifer is conclusively explained by a game narrator.

- **Iron Legions:** led by **Archduke Azrakh**, an honour-bound infernal conqueror who finds Vhal's forged right of conquest insulting. Infernal Thralls march behind Ravager Beasts and Hellreavers. His promise is order through dominion; his temptation is to become an oppressor while claiming to liberate Hell.
- **Blood Brood:** led by **Broodmother Tzura**. Her swarms and Skitters are bred in Birthing Pits. Tzura is a formidable strategist with a genuine wish to stop her children being endlessly consumed in other lords' wars. She is also willing to consume cities for survival.
- **Court of Whispers:** led by **Chancellor Vhal**, with **Lady Nerezza**, a disillusioned Hexer, as the player's dangerous companion. The Court wages war with intelligence, counterfeit orders, infiltrators and binding contracts. Some within it seek a genuine release from the infernal hierarchy; Vhal seeks only to take its throne.

**Current Demon roster woven into missions:** Thrall, Cinderling, Ravager Beast, Skitter, Hellreaver, Hexer; Furnace Hearts, Maw of Want, Birthing Pits, Flesh Forges, Scrying Spires and Forbidden Archives.

**Hell's playable moral choice:** is abolishing chains an act of emancipation if the liberated become the new jailers?

### The Witness Kin and other nonplayable powers
**The Witness Kin** are diverse human communities who preserve fragments of ancient languages and memory rites. Some have distant Nephilim ancestry, some are adopted keepers of the tradition, some know nothing of the cause of their inherited dreams. They are never a fourth selectable super-faction and never a species of inherently superior saviours. They can be defended, displaced, blackmailed or heard.

- **Tamar Rell:** mortal witness-librarian; carries a fragmented oral map, not a physical superweapon. Her testimony is the central continuity anchor across all campaigns. She survives until the shared Harrow climax; her trust may vary.
- **The Two Witnesses:** a pair of independent signal broadcasters who document crimes by *all three sides*. They echo Revelation's two-witness imagery but are original characters, **Sister Calen** and **Observer Niko**. Their testimony disrupts both propaganda machines.
- **The Pale Rider / Wormwood / the Abyssal Locusts:** are apocalyptic *conditions and signs* appearing as neutral, telegraphed environmental events; they do not constitute a secretly playable faction. The Pale Rider is a terrifying vision, not a recruitable tank.

## 5. CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE AND SHARED CHRONOLOGY

**Expanded canonical plan: 75 missions, exactly 25 per major faction.** The full, numbered, objective-by-objective specification lives in **[CAMPAIGN_MISSIONS.md](CAMPAIGN_MISSIONS.md)**. This bible describes the setting and character arcs; the mission atlas is authoritative for mission numbering, detailed objectives, fail states and crossover references. The original eight mission titles per campaign survive in their relative story order, with the final titles now at H25, A25 and D25.

The campaigns share one broad history but each tells a different, sometimes mistaken account. Numbering is local to the selected campaign: H07 and A08 describe the same Broken Choir conflict from opposite sides. The final Harrow battles H25, A25 and D25 are **alternative victories**, never all true simultaneously.

| Act | Missions in each campaign | Narrative focus | RTS progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. The Breaking | 01–05 | Fall, rescue, incursion and first crystal | MCV deployment, power, refinery, infantry and initial vehicles |
| II. The War of Claims | 06–10 | Coercive edicts, sieges and the fight for territory | Ambushes, artillery, subfaction exclusives and harassment |
| III. The False Edicts | 11–15 | Institutional betrayals, war crimes and forged authority | Multi-front fights, regional hazards and intelligence |
| IV. The Witness Ledger | 16–20 | Restoring the ancient consent clause | Escorts, archive defenses, evidence and changing alliances |
| V. The Harrow of Dawn | 21–25 | Three invasions and the irreversible final choice | Large-scale logistics, siege warfare and planned superweapon finales |

**Gameplay invariant:** Sevenfall remains a classic Command & Conquer style RTS. Most missions require a base and **finite, physical Veilglass extraction** with returns to the matching faction refinery. No campaign introduces passive strategic-point requisition. Civilian escorts and scripted infiltrations are specific mission variants, not the core economy. All 75 missions are **story specifications** rather than already implemented OpenRA gameplay.

## 6. CAMPAIGN I: ASHES OF EARTH (HUMANS)

**25 missions: H01–H25.** The player leads the Last Dominion of Man, with Bastion Directorate, Free Earth Union or Ashen Covenant perspectives. The story starts with **The Day the Sky Broke** and **Last Light**, reaches real harvesting at **The Glass Fields (H03)**, contests **Do Not Look Up (H07)**, defends **The Endless Descent (H10)**, exposes **The False Sanctuary (H14)** and **Broken Covenant (H19)**, and ends at **We Remain (H25)**. Voss's military control, Awan's communal sovereignty and Vey's research ethics complicate every victory. The final decision is **Open Earth** or **Iron Earth**.

[See all 25 human mission briefs and objectives.](CAMPAIGN_MISSIONS.md#campaign-i-ashes-of-earth-humans)

### Human opening cinematic (script extract)
**BLACK.** A siren gives way to a sound like hundreds of cathedral bells striking underwater. Every phone in Crown Vale shows the same unreadable symbol. Across the skyline, a line of gold opens in the clouds. Below it, black iron begins climbing out of the streets.

**MARA VOSS (radio):** "The shelters are full. The rail lines are gone. Tell me what we still have."

**IDRIS AWAN:** "Three generators. Two tanks that start when threatened. And people who won't leave their neighbours."

**MARA:** "Then we have an army."

**TITLE:** ASHES OF EARTH.

## 9. THE SAME BATTLE, THREE UNRELIABLE ACCOUNTS

The **[75-mission atlas](CAMPAIGN_MISSIONS.md#shared-events-consistency-locks)** is the authoritative shared-event table. The principal crossovers are:

1. **H07 / A08, Broken Choir inspection:** the human account is an occupation; the angelic account is a containment mission. Both witness the forged authorization. D14 describes the Court's aftermath and misinformation.
2. **H10 / A10 / D12, Ashen Causeway:** civilians attempt to flee a demonic siege, defended by a human convoy screen and an independent angelic guardian. Optional rescue choices shift dialogue, not core history.
3. **A06 / D05, Sable Crown:** celestial raiders destroy an outer siege forge; the demonic player defends the main foundry. The accounts describe distinct locations of one complex, so they can both occur.
4. **H14 / D10, Salt Meridian:** coercive Court contracts are the same scheme discovered on different dates. Angels uncover the environmental consequences in A14.
5. **H16–H20 / A16–A20 / D16–D20:** each side independently reconstructs the **same missing consent clause**, from different records and witnesses.
6. **H25 / A25 / D25:** each is an *alternate timeline* after the common Harrow siege, with two ethical conclusions for its victor.

The public website should not spoil the forged orders before the intended midpoint. Keep spoiler-tagged dialogue, localization and cutscene notes separate from public marketing summaries.

## 10. THE THREE ENDING FAMILIES

Every final mission awards the player a victory through recognisable RTS action, then asks them what kind of peace the army will enforce. These are **six ending variants** grouped into three campaign finales, not six extra missions.

| Campaign | Cooperative / liberation ending | Domination / control ending |
|---|---|---|
| Humanity | **Open Earth:** breaches severed, elected settlements govern rebuilding, surviving Angels/Demons may petition for asylum | **Iron Earth:** Lazarus monopolized by martial command; humans win independence but lose much of their civic liberty |
| Angels | **The Open Gate:** cease coercive edicts, protect by invitation, investigate the forged hierarchy | **Radiant Quarantine:** the world survives but remains a supervised celestial fortress |
| Demons | **Unbound Fire:** infernal command chains broken, dangerous emancipation and no guaranteed peace | **Crown of Ash:** the victor becomes the very infernal tyrant whose captivity they denounced |

**Default continuity for a hypothetical sequel:** record that the Seventh Silence occurred, the conspiracy was exposed, and the battle for the Harrow happened; leave its political winner unresolved unless a specific campaign ending is selected. This supports campaigns that can be played in any order.

### Final words from Tamar Rell
> "You all brought a verdict. None of you thought to ask the living for a witness."

### Ending sample: Humanity / Open Earth
At sunrise, the last Harvester returns through burnt streets. The soldiers do not cheer; they take off their helmets to hear birds returning to the rooftops. No proclamation descends from Heaven. No king rises from Hell. A child asks who owns the crystal fields. The council answers: no one without permission from the people who must live beside them.

### Ending sample: Angels / Radiant Quarantine
Every wound in the sky closes. The dead are counted. The living are fed. Above every rebuilt city hangs a bright, unwavering ring. Crime and famine diminish. So do elections, borders and the right to say no. The Host calls this a miracle. Beneath the Choir, a mortal keeps a ledger of forbidden questions.

### Ending sample: Demons / Unbound Fire
The Ninth Mouth opens inward. Across the infernal plains, iron collars collapse into dust. Some freed soldiers kneel from habit; others fight for food; a few turn back toward Earth to keep the new road open. No freedom comes with instructions. A demon child, born in a Birthing Pit and never given a name, chooses one.

## 11. HOW THE NINE SUBFACTIONS CHANGE THE STORY

Selecting a subfaction should matter without multiplying the main story into 225 distinct maps. Each parent campaign has 25 missions (75 total), with a distinct **named advisor**, **exclusive existing unit**, **additional optional objective**, **dialogue position**, **ending epilogue line** and **one field modifier** per choice. Exclusive mechanics beyond the prototype are planned, not shipped.

| Subfaction | Narrative intervention | Prototype exclusive | Planned campaign effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seraphic Synod | Seraviel demands obedience; A08 quarantine is framed as holy procedure | Seraphim | Strong assault / costly defensive errors |
| Order of the Gate | Orathiel prioritizes evacuees and threshold defence | Gatekeeper | Fortify/refugee objective and slow shielding |
| The Unshackled | Maeron exposes the forged order earliest but risks court-martial | Penitent | Alternate infiltration route and early harassment |
| Bastion Directorate | Voss can save a city by martial requisition | Siege Cannon | Extra siege option with settlement trust cost |
| Free Earth Union | Idris builds consent through scattered civilian coalitions | Wasteland Ranger | Faster scouting and extra rescue objective |
| Ashen Covenant | Elian can read the archives, but research ethics are contested | Ash Templar | Anti-entity tools, costly evidence choices |
| Iron Legions | Azrakh insists contracts be challenged in open war | Hellreaver | Honour duel/fortress breakout |
| Blood Brood | Tzura wants freedom for her offspring, not only victory | Skitter | Swarm extraction / protecting vulnerable broods |
| Court of Whispers | Nerezza undermines Vhal's lies from within | Hexer | Stealth/revelation route; command betrayal risk |

**Explicit rule:** no faction should have to massacre civilians to earn a basic ending, and compassion cannot simply mean a free economic buff. Offer tactical costs, alternate paths, visible consequences and intelligible enemy counterplay.

## 13. FINAL NARRATIVE NORTH STAR

An Angel looks at a ruined city and sees a people to rescue even if they resist. A Demon sees a realm where nobody need obey Hell's ancient masters, if only someone will grant permission to rebel. A Human looks at both armies and understands the unthinkable: the end of the world has not abolished ordinary responsibility.

The first question in Sevenfall is **"Who owns Creation?"**

The last question is harder:

**"Who has the right to answer for everyone else?"**
