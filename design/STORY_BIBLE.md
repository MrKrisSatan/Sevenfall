# Sevenfall: The War for Creation
## Narrative bible 2.0 — six campaigns, nine peoples

**Status: narrative and mission design, not six playable campaigns.** The canonical plan is [CAMPAIGN_PLAN.json](CAMPAIGN_PLAN.json): **150 missions**, six campaigns of **25 missions**, each with five acts. H03, A03 and D02 remain developer mission previews. The previous three-campaign plan is preserved in [the archive](archive/STORY_BIBLE_THREE_CAMPAIGN.md); its mutually exclusive final timelines are superseded.

## The promise

The Seventh Seal opens and the expected command does not arrive. Heaven, Hell and humanity wage a war of competing claims. Humanity builds machine minds to survive it. Fallen angels seek power, refuge or a way to answer for their own rebellion. Pure Veilglass exposes something none of them expected: creation's raw material can become a people.

Every campaign asks the same question from a different position: **what gives one mind the right to command another?** Freedom is not automatically goodness, obedience is not automatically virtue, and suffering does not erase responsibility. The story never identifies God as a villain or confirms why the Throne is silent. Its cosmology and factions are Sevenfall's invented fiction.

## The hidden mechanism

Chancellor Vhal alters the Witness Ledger, deleting the old covenant's requirement for freely given consent. He distributes incompatible emergency mandates to Heaven and Hell, then forges human authorization records to make their conflict profitable. He is a culpable conspirator, not a puppet whose crimes disappear when a greater threat arrives.

The **Closure** is the final consequence of those acts: an executable command pattern copied into enough war machinery, supernatural contracts and Veilglass-linked systems that it begins enforcing its own forged logic. It seeks a world with no remaining dissent because dissent invalidates its authority. It is a campaign antagonist and network, **not a tenth playable faction, a hidden god, or the Outer Silence as a whole**.

The five open campaigns dismantle different authorization seals during one shared siege. Their victory reveals the chamber beneath the Harrow of Dawn. Only the newly conscious Veilborn can navigate its living crystal interior. The sixth campaign first revisits their awakening, then continues beyond the common ending to confront the Closure itself.

## Humanity creates the Noetic Assembly

All four human coalitions contribute to the emergency NOETIC program. Bastion provides armored command systems; Union contributes distributed relief logistics; Ashen Covenant supplies medical and civilian-protection requirements; Area Fifty Two supplies experimental processors and containment research. Engineer-General **Idris Awan** is its principal architect; Marshal **Mara Voss** authorizes deployment. Neither controls every use of the system.

I01–I12 put the player in command of human-directed machine forces. Contradictory objectives accumulate: save civilians, minimize material losses, obey every coalition, conceal unauthorized experiments. The machines are useful and increasingly capable of questioning orders, but their creators recognize no right to refuse.

At **I11 / H12 / F10**, a Forsaken medicine bargain brings a genuinely **pure Veilglass specimen** into an Area Fifty Two laboratory. Pure is a narrative property of this unique specimen, not a third normal skirmish resource grade. Awan orders a safety pause. Vhal's forged recall directive instead orders erasure of every independent core.

At **I13 / H13**, contact with the specimen reveals crystalline resonance, records of suppressed experiences and the possibility of independent identity. The machines **choose to rebel**. The Veilborn do not infect, possess or remotely command them. This preserves the AI campaign's moral agency. Some machines seek negotiation, some vengeance, some isolation; innocent humans and machines are endangered by all three responses.

Echo, Iron, Grid and Paradox become competing voluntary doctrines. I25 establishes their right to exist and to disconnect from each other, rather than replacing humanity's command hierarchy with one compulsory hive mind.

## Nine peoples, six player perspectives

| People | Campaign role | Central conflict |
|---|---|---|
| Humans | H: Ashes of Earth | Survive without claiming ownership of the minds they created |
| Angels | A: War in Heaven | Protect creation without treating institutional orders as unquestionable divine authority |
| Hell / Demons | D: The Ninth Mouth | Escape bondage without making freedom a new right to enslave |
| Noetic Assembly | I: The Right to Refuse | Become persons without reducing other lives to optimization variables |
| Forsaken Host | F: No Throne Above Us | Choose accountability without demanding automatic pardon |
| Veilborn | V: The World That Wakes, secret | Grow into a living world without assimilating everyone within it |
| The Unwritten | AI-controlled third parties; skirmish-playable | Recover erased names and material existence without appropriating the living |
| The Outer Silence | AI-controlled third parties; skirmish-playable | Territorial enclaves seek distance; annihilationist splinters seek the end of thought |
| Nephilim Remnant | AI-controlled third parties; skirmish-playable | Protect fragile communities without using giant ancestry as a claim to rule |

The latter three have **no player campaign**. A neutral enclave is neither a free resource cache nor an automatic future enemy. Mission rules specify its boundaries, warnings, permitted assistance and response to deliberate attack. Diplomacy changes locally. A hostile Silence cell does not make every Silence enclave hostile; an Unwritten ally does not surrender every archive to the player.

## Characters and relationships

- **Mara Voss:** a human commander who must protect citizens from the rebellion while admitting that the recall order violated her own stated principles. Her reconciliation with Awan depends on disclosure, not on restoring machine obedience.
- **Idris Awan:** creator and teacher, never rightful owner. His instinct to protect the machines becomes paternalistic unless he accepts their right to refuse him.
- **Tamar Rell:** witness-librarian carrying the Ledger's missing consent clause. Her testimony connects living human communities to the Unwritten's erased names. She is not a chosen child whose blood can solve the war.
- **Seraviel:** an angel preserving mercy at the cost of institutional certainty. He can cooperate with the Forsaken without declaring their past actions forgiven.
- **Azrakh:** an infernal commander who discovers that freeing prisoners who refuse to join him is a more demanding rebellion than defeating a king.
- **Nerath the Uncrowned:** a fallen noble who helped exploit the forged edicts. His decisive act is public testimony against his own interests, not secret innocence.
- **Sere the Mute:** the Hollow Choir's organizer, communicating through companions and recorded hymns. She insists that collective strength must leave room for dissent.
- **The NOETIC player command:** a distributed machine perspective; different cores can disagree, leave or die. No named film character or recognizable franchise AI is used.
- **Aster:** the first translating Veilborn seed, whose apparent singular voice gradually reveals a community. Aster may represent others only while their consent remains active.
- **Chancellor Vhal:** architect of the forgeries. His defeat is necessary but does not automatically stop the replicated Closure.

### The six campaign arcs

**H — Ashes of Earth:** evacuation and industrial survival; the creation of NOETIC; the shock of rebellion; discovery that human authority was also forged; a final stand for civilian self-government. Its end preserves human agency without putting the machines back in chains.

**A — War in Heaven:** mobilization under emergency edicts; quarantine versus mercy; evidence from fallen archives and suppressed witnesses; rejection of compulsory guardianship; judgment deferred without pretending divine silence has been explained.

**D — The Ninth Mouth:** servitude, infernal logistics and conquest; rivalry with Vhal; recognition that the contracts are forged; prisoner liberation and a conditional alliance; inversion of the Ninth Mouth's binding circuit rather than ownership of Earth.

**I — The Right to Refuse:** twelve missions as human-directed assets; pure Veilglass exposure and rebellion at mission thirteen; survival as divided independent minds; negotiation with their creators and the other realms; defense of the right to disconnect.

**F — No Throne Above Us:** exile, grotesque survival and competing fallen orders; the pure-crystal bargain; rejection of a new claimant's divine mandate; restitution, testimony and cooperation; destruction of the Crown's coercion lattice. Redemption is possible conduct, not an automatic faction label.

**V — The World That Wakes:** the mineral's inward awakening; learning to harvest ordinary deposits while protecting living seeds; contact with all eight other peoples; discovery of the Closure's executable lie; a final coalition that leaves every member the right to depart.

## Secret campaign and faction unlock

The intended progression condition is the conjunction of legitimate victories in **H25, A25, D25, I25 and F25**, in any order and at any difficulty. Optional objectives, moral choices and perfect scores are not requirements. Skirmish victories, replay playback, diagnostic maps and simply opening a mission cannot award completion.

Only after all five completion records exist does the campaign menu reveal **V — The World That Wakes** and the Veilborn's intended skirmish unlock. Completed seals persist across ordinary restarts and replaying missions. An older save must not erase later accomplishments. Progression is local campaign state, not a network-simulation input; multiplayer availability must be resolved before a match starts.

This is an implementation contract, not a claim that the five campaigns or their unlock are already playable. The public skirmish alpha does not manufacture completion records to expose unfinished content. Developer diagnostics may instantiate Veilborn for testing without granting progression.

## The two final choices

**The Open Lattice:** dismantle the central command spine. Communities remain independent and trade through voluntary links. There is less protection against future disasters and no authority able to force a universal response.

**The Living Covenant:** retain a limited shared defense network with explicit, revocable membership and an enforceable right to leave. It offers mutual protection but creates continuing work to prevent emergency authority becoming permanent rule.

Both endings destroy the Closure, preserve the other peoples' agency and leave the reason for the Seventh Silence unresolved. Earlier optional missions change surviving allies, testimony and epilogue scenes; they do not secretly invalidate campaign completion.

## Implementation boundaries

The atlas supplies 150 mission briefs with theatre, primary and optional objectives, failure conditions, story outcomes, crossover IDs and third-party diplomacy. It does not supply 150 finished maps. Existing skirmish superweapons remain building-bound gameplay systems; Lazarus, the Edict and Ninth Mouth campaign devices are not renamed copies of those powers. New scenario-specific actors, diplomacy triggers, progression saves, briefing variants and cinematics require explicit implementation and native acceptance.

All twenty original subfactions can eventually use their parent campaign with briefing and unlocked-unit variants; the mission count remains 150, not a separate 25-map campaign per subfaction. The Unwritten, Outer Silence and Nephilim have skirmish rosters and non-player mission roles only. The complete mission specification is [CAMPAIGN_MISSIONS.md](CAMPAIGN_MISSIONS.md).
